## 1. Where they are right and we were wrong

**Mary Jane Carver's parents.** This tree carried Elias Carver + Laura Story at *probable*,
and the only thing holding it up was her Find a Grave memorial — which is their own S54. A
competing household exists (Rev. James Monroe Carver + Susan Celester Flanders) and this tree
had never mentioned it. Coffee County marriage indexes show both couples existed, which proves
two households and settles nothing. Under this project's own rule — record conflicts, never
smooth them — that conflict should have been on the page and was not. **It is now.**
Resolves with her 1982 death record or her own childhood household in 1910/1920.

**A grading nuance on Nealie.** Her link was graded confirmed across *both* parents on one
mark. The mother is confirmed; the father is not. That is now split in the text.

## 2. Where we are right and their gaps close

**Jack Luke's parents — their biggest open gap, and it is already closed here.**
Jack appears as "John," age 3, in the **1900 Irwin County household of Lucius and Narcessia
Luke**, and as "John C W Luke," age 12, in **1910**. Two federal censuses placing him as a
child in that house. Their S06/S08/S24–S26 were chasing John J. Luke (1846–1908) out of a
cemetery transcription and a probate index — the wrong man entirely.
And the line runs one further: **Lucius L. Luke's 1920 Georgia death certificate no. 18075**
names his own parents as *R. B. Luke* and *Jane Yawn*.

> **Same-name trap both files must log.** Their S09 "Lucious Luke" is Jack's **son** (Elmer's
> brother). Our Lucius L. Luke (1872–1920) is Jack's **father**. A grandson named for his
> grandfather. Merging them on name inverts a generation.

**Clyde McMillan — their open question is answered.** They hold the 8 Sep 1918
Johnnie Luke–Clyde McMillan marriage as plausible-not-confirmed, worried by a 1920 census
entry pairing a Clyde Luke, 18, with a Wm L Luke. Reading the Irwin County register straight
through settles it: **"LUKE W. L. — HANLEY CLYDE — 06/23/1918"** sits three months earlier in
the same index. Two different women named Clyde, two different Lukes. The 1920 household is
W. L. Luke and Clyde Hanley and says nothing about Jack.
**Richard Cleo Luke Sr. is therefore Jack's son and Elmer's half-brother, not a nephew.**

**The Mercer line — their largest gap, and we hold the records they ask for.**
Their standing rules say Ida's maiden name is unknown, that "Ida Frances Brown" must not be
used, and that "Charles Robert" is only a search candidate. All three can be lifted:
- **1910 census, Militia District 1224, Clinch County** — Chas R. Mercer 39, Ida 36, both
  "m 19", with **Sally, daughter, aged 8**.
- **Clinch County Marriage Book 1889–1899, page 113** — Charles R. Mercer to **Ida Brown**;
  licence 21 May by Ordinary George Cornelius, joined 24 May by J. A. Osteen. (The page
  contradicts itself on the year — 1892 in figures, "Eighteen Hundred and Ninety one" in
  words — recorded unresolved here.)
- **Georgia death certificate 18749**, 3 Sep 1943, Pearson — Charlie's own, naming father
  **Jim Mercer** and mother **Sarah Robinson**, informant his eldest son L. R. Mercer.

Their S94/S95 aggregate snippets were right, but they had no way to know it. The tree runs
two further generations from there: Ida's parents James Brown and Sallie Hughes, and Sallie's
parents William H. and Nancy Hughes of South Carolina.

**"Martha Ann Ricketson."** Their file uses that name throughout. Her headstone reads
Martha **"Mattie"** Ricketson — there is no Ann; her mother was Martha *Amanda* Overstreet,
which is the likely source of the error. Their UNPROVED status is also too harsh: the **1910
census has her father John Rickerson living in the household, labelled by the enumerator
"maternal grandfather."** That is a primary record of the relationship, not a compiled lead.

**Nealie's parents.** Their S18 is a Find a Grave contributor link. The **1910 census of
Levyville, Levy County, Florida (ED 82, sheet 5B)** enters *Abagil Hinton, head, widowed, 26*
with Otelia 9, James 6, **Neal 2** and Mack 1 — the mother–daughter relationship recorded by
a federal enumerator. The father remains inference: Abby is widowed by April 1910 and the
baby is named Mack.

## 3. The do-not-merge rule neither file had

**There are two entirely unrelated Giddens families in this tree.**
Abby Johns Hinton **Giddens** remarried a Giddens after Mackie Hinton died — which is why
their S18 memorial carries that surname, and why **Ben Giddens, 11**, appears in the 1930
J. W. Luke household as Nealie's half-brother. That is the *paternal* side.
**Joe Edd Giddens of Atkinson County is the maternal side.** Same surname, no relationship.
Merging on the name alone fuses two families.

## 4. The orphanage testimony — the most valuable thing in their document

Their S93: Joe Edd **grew up in an orphanage and never knew his parents.**

It is the first account that fits what the records do: no 1900 census, no 1910, no 1920,
no WWI draft card, and a man appearing fully formed at thirty-eight in 1930.

**But it does not explain the silence — it predicts the opposite.** Institutions were
enumerated. A boy in a Georgia orphans' home in 1900 or 1910 appears on the population
schedule by name, as an inmate. All 56 Giddens/Gidden/Giddins/Giddings/Gedings/Gideon
children recorded anywhere in Georgia in the 1900 census with a birth in 1893–94 have been
read, and none is a November 1893 boy, institutional or otherwise.

So the testimony plus the negatives point hard at the caveat the family attached themselves:
**it does not establish that Giddens was his birth surname.** A child raised in an institution
or placed out to a family can easily carry a name that was not his father's.

That changes the question from *who were Joe Edd Giddens's parents* to **which boy, under
whatever name, became Joe Edd Giddens** — and the records that answer it are institutional
admission and placing-out registers, mostly in denominational archives rather than online:
the Georgia Baptist Orphans' Home, the Methodist home at Decatur, the Hephzibah home near
Augusta, the Georgia Industrial Home at Macon, the Appleton Church Home.

**The owed search has now been run — and it is a fourth clean negative.**
All **50** male Giddens/Gidden/Giddins/Giddings/Giddons/Gideon children recorded anywhere in
Georgia in the **1910 census** with a birth 1892–96 were listed. **Every one is entered in a
family with a parent named.** Not one is an inmate, ward, boarder or unattached boy — which
matters, because an institutional child is enumerated with the parent columns *empty* and is
therefore unusually easy to spot in an index. The only Joseph in the set is the Clinch County
boy, son of the widow Fanny, already eliminated.

**The count is now four: no 1900, no 1910, no 1920, no draft card**, searched under every
spelling the surname takes. A man who was somewhere for thirty-eight years and appears in none
of them was not being written down as *Giddens*. Taken with the orphanage account, that stops
being a guess and becomes what the records are saying.

## 5. Assessment of the document itself

**Its method is genuinely good.** Explicit evidence labels, stable source IDs, do-not-merge
rules, and the principle most hobby trees ignore — *repeated copies of one obituary are one
evidentiary event, not multiple independent witnesses* — which it actually applies (S40/S41,
S49/S50, S90/S91). It also refuses to connect the Paulk/Young branch it researched, which is
the right call and a disciplined one.

**Its evidence base is thin, and the file is honest about why.** The register is overwhelmingly
Find a Grave memorials, FamilySearch *compiled profiles* (user-edited trees, not records),
obituaries and indexes — which by its own rules are leads. Very few inspected originals.
Several entries record that the page was never opened at all: S26, S36, S56, S57, S72
("page open failed"), S67, S68 ("direct access 403"), S29 ("browser access denied").

**The gap between the two files is index versus image.** Where this project went to the sheet,
the answer changed — three times in one day: Ruth Brown's age is 11/12 not 1/12; Hughes was
indexed "Hugh's"; Sallie was indexed "Lacey," which is why her parents sat unproven for years.

Its open gaps are mostly closable with records already read here.

# Second pass — records found 17 September

Four gaps closed, all with records rather than compiled sources.

## 1. Mary Jane Carver's parents — CONFIRMED, and both files were half right

The conflict I recorded that morning is settled, and it took a state record to do it.

- **1920 census, Douglas, Coffee County** — the household of **Elias Carver** and wife Laura,
  with **Mary J., Matilda, Levadar and Ben**. All four were already on this tree as siblings,
  assembled one at a time from elsewhere: Levada off the 1950 sheet in Elias's own house, Ben
  from a household a few doors away. The 1920 page puts them together under their parents.
  This is the record this tree's own page said was missing.
- **Matilda Carver McKinnon's Georgia death certificate**, died 12 June 1942, District 748,
  Coffee County — **parents Elias Carver and Laura Newbern**. Buried Carver Baptist Church
  Cemetery, Douglas, the same ground as Elias and Laura.

**So our Elias + Laura was right and their James Monroe Carver + Susan Flanders was wrong —
but their doubt was entirely reasonable**, because the rival household is real. The 1910
census of Pearson holds James M. Carver Jr and Susan C. with a *Mary J.* born 1900 **and a
Matilda M.** born 1902. Two unrelated Carver households in one county, each with a Mary Jane
and a Matilda of nearly the same age. Neither file could have known without the certificate.

Link upgraded probable → confirmed. Residual inference stated on the page: no document read
here says the Mary J. of 1920 is the woman who married Monroe McKinnon in 1922. Her 1982
death record closes that inch.

## 2. Laura's maiden name — a new conflict, and the better record disagrees with us

Matilda's certificate gives her mother as **Laura Newbern**. This tree says **Laura Story**,
from a memorial. *Newbern* is a state record; *Story* has never been read off a record at all.
But the mother's-maiden-name box is the field informants get wrong most often, and Matilda's
informant was her husband reporting on his mother-in-law. **Both stand; neither is preferred.**
The Coffee County marriage return of 24 December 1899 would settle it.

## 3. The Matilda testimony — resolved, and the family were very nearly right

**Matilda Carver McKinnon (1906–1942) was Mary Jane's sister.** She married **Leon McKinnon**;
Mary Jane married **Monroe McKinnon**. **Two Carver sisters married two McKinnon men** — which
is why a Matilda McKinnon looms so large in this family's memory, and why the relationship was
easy to misplace. She was Andrew's aunt twice over: his mother's sister, and married into his
father's name. She died when Andrew was five.

"Papa Andrew his granny was Matilda McKinnon" was right about the woman, the surname and the
family, and one step out on the generation — the most ordinary error in oral history. The other
file predicted exactly this shape without being able to prove it.

## 4. Mattie Cribb's parents — CONFIRMED, a whole new generation

Their PROBABLE lead was right, and the census they pointed at without opening is what proves it.

- **1920, Pearson, Atkinson County** — Mattie, ~9, with **Martha** and siblings Lexie, Annie B.,
  Louis, Addie M., under a head the index reads **"Don Cribb."**
- **1910, McAlpin, Suwannee County, Florida** — **David B. Cribbs**, b. ~1875 in Georgia, wife
  **Martha**, children **Mattie, Annie B., Louis J.**

Same wife, same three children, ten years apart. **"Don" is the indexer reading *D. B.*** —
initials in a clerk's hand. David Benjamin Cribbs and Martha are now on the chart.

A pattern falls out of it: the Cribbses crossed from **Suwannee County, Florida** into Atkinson
County between 1910 and 1920 — the same Florida-to-south-Georgia move the Lukes and Hintons made
from Levy County a decade later. Two of the four great-grandparent lines came north over that
border.

**Caveat carried on the page:** both Cribbs households are so far read at the index level. The
sheets have not been opened, and the 1920 given name is exactly what only an image settles.

# Third pass — the 1891/1892 marriage conflict is settled

## The headline: three records against one

The Clinch County marriage book contradicted itself about the year Charles R. Mercer married
Ida Brown — **1892 in figures** at the top of page 113, **"Eighteen Hundred and Ninety one"**
written out in the minister's return beneath it. This tree recorded that unresolved and said
in as many words that there was "no way to choose between them without a third."

**The third record is the 1900 census of Magnolia, Clinch County**, which enumerates Charles R.
Mercer and Ida together and puts the marriage at **1891** — that schedule asked couples outright
how many years they had been married. So:

| Record | Year it gives |
|---|---|
| Minister's return, in words | 1891 |
| 1900 census (years married) | 1891 |
| 1910 census ("m 19") | 1891 |
| Licence, in figures | 1892 |

**Three to one, and the one is the kind that slips** — a clerk writing "1892" in a book he had
just begun filling with 1892 entries. That also disposes of the argument from the neighbouring
pages being 1892: those are the very thing that would have carried his pen. **The wedding was
May 1891.**

## Also found

- **1900 census, Magnolia, Clinch County** — Charles R. and Ida Mercer with **George, Robert M.,
  Maud and William D.** Robert M. is new to this tree. Ida's birth given as April 1874, matching
  her headstone.
- **George Albert McKinnon's death record** — Georgia Deaths 1928–43, died **1932, Militia
  District 748, Coffee County**, born about 1876. No parents in the index, so it does not reach
  further back; the certificate image might.
- **Janie Ruth Yawn Luke** — exact dates at last: born **14 March 1855**, died **1 November
  1943**. The tree had only "about 1855" and "1943."
- **Lucius Luke's 1920 certificate cross-confirmed** independently in the state index: *R. B.
  Luke — spouse Jane Yawn — children Lucius Luke.*
- **"Ida Brown Mercer"** is how the state indexed her on Charlie's 1943 certificate — a third
  independent attestation of the Brown maiden name, after the marriage book and the 1910 census.

## Clean negatives from this pass — recorded so nobody repeats them

- **Ida Frances Brown has no death certificate, and there is a plain reason.** She died
  **25 January 1919**; Georgia began statewide death registration *that month*, and rural
  compliance in the first year was near nil. Nothing for her in the state's 1914–27 collection.
  That certificate would have named her parents and closed the last gap on the Brown line. It
  was never written.
- **No Hinton household anywhere in Levy County, Florida in 1920.** The family left between
  1910 and 1920 — consistent with Nealie being in Irwin County, Georgia by 1930.
- **No death certificate found for Jane Yawn Luke** (d. 1 Nov 1943) despite the collection
  covering that year, under Luke or Yawn.
- **The Georgia Archives' digitised death series is patchy for rural 1919–24.** No Martha Amanda
  Overstreet (d. ~1921), no Mary Jane Durham (d. 1921), no James "Jim" McKinnon (d. 1924). Their
  certificates may exist unfilmed; the online collection is the digitised subset, not the series.

## Method note worth keeping

The systematic move that produced most of this: **list every ancestor who died in Georgia
between 1919 and 1943, because their death certificates are free and name both parents.** That
list is short, and working it is far more efficient than name-searching. The two windows are
served by different collections — Georgia Archives (1919–27, with father and mother exposed
directly in the item metadata via its CONTENTdm API) and FamilySearch's Georgia Deaths 1928–43.

# Fifth pass — Elias Carver's parents, and a correction to our own reasoning

## The generation is found

**1880 census, Militia District 1026, Coffee County** — **Joshua Carver**, about 30, his wife
**Amanda**, and three children: **Elizer Jane**, **Elias** (three, born 1877) and **Leander**.

1880 is the first federal census that asked each person's **relationship to the head of the
house** outright. So this is not an inference from who was sleeping where — it is the enumerator
writing *son* against Elias's name. Joshua and Amanda Carver go onto the chart.

## The correction — our inference was wrong at face value

This page had reasoned from Uncle Allen Carver's 1916 obituary that Elias's father must be one
of **Jesse, Vincent, Joe or Pink Carver**, with **James Carver** as candidate grandfather.

**Joshua is not among those four.** He might be the obituary's *Joe* — Joshua does shorten that
way — but nothing read here says so, and the 1880 sheet points the other way (see below). The
four-brother field is marked superseded on the page rather than deleted, because the reasoning
was sound and the record beat it.

## Why the grandfather is still missing, and where he is

Living with Joshua in 1880 is **Pheby**, entered as his **mother**, under a surname the index
reads as *Sping* — almost certainly Spring or near it — together with a **Mathew** of that
surname entered as Joshua's **brother**.

A widow living with a grown son under a second husband's name, with a half-brother beside her.
That is the ordinary shape of it, and it means **Joshua's father died before 1880 and this sheet
does not name him**. It also argues against James Carver: if James were Joshua's father, his
widow would more likely still be a Carver.

Joshua is in **Coffee County in 1870** too, aged about twenty — but 1870 recorded no
relationships at all, only who slept under which roof, so that page cannot be read for parentage
without the image.

**Pheby is the thread.** Find her in 1870 or 1860 and Joshua's father should be in the same
household.

## Also noted

- Elias gains two siblings: **Elizer Jane** and **Leander Carver**.
- **Amanda's maiden name is not recoverable from the census** — it never asked. The Coffee County
  marriage books would have it, and they are the same filmed series that produced the
  Mercer–Brown page and the McKinnon–Carver licence already on this site.
- A **James J. Carver** household sits in the same District 1026 in 1880, with a daughter *Izer
  Jane* alongside Joshua's *Elizer Jane*, and a *Matilda*. Carver naming in this county repeats
  hard, which is exactly why the Mary Jane / Matilda confusion happened two generations later.

Tree is now **57 people, 31 parent links, ten generations**.

# Sixth pass — 17 September 2026, evening
### Two old questions bounded: Laura's maiden name, and whether Joshua belongs to James Carver

## Laura Carver — the marriage record has now actually been read

The tree has carried a conflict since the Mary Jane confirmation: the Georgia death index for
Elias Carver (1928–1943) lists his spouse as **Laura Newbern**, while a Find a Grave memorial
called her **Laura Story**. A memorial is not evidence, so the page said so and left it open.

That is now settled in favour of a record rather than a memorial. Georgia's **county marriage
index for Coffee County** carries the entry directly:

> **Elias Carver — 24 December 1899 — Coffee, Georgia — Laura Story**

That is a county clerk's record of a licence returned, made at the time of the event by someone
with no interest in the outcome. It outranks a death index entry, which was filled in by whoever
reported the death thirty-odd years later, and it outranks a memorial absolutely.

**Story is now preferred.** Newbern stays visible on the page, because a state certificate is not
nothing and the discrepancy is itself a fact about the record set.

### Both surnames were then tested, and both came up empty

Preferring a name is not the same as finding the woman. Two searches were run to try to put her in
a childhood household:

- **No Laura Story born about 1877 anywhere in Georgia in the 1880 census.** Not in Coffee, not
  in the surrounding counties, not in the state.
- **No Newbern or Newborn household in Coffee County in 1880 at all.** The surname simply is not
  there in that census year.
- **No Story–Newbern marriage** anywhere in the Georgia index — which kills the tidy hypothesis
  that she was a Newbern widow who remarried a Story, or the reverse.

So the memorial's claim that her parents were **Josiah Story and Jincy Willis** rests on nothing
reachable. It has been written onto the page as **unsupported** rather than quietly copied, which
is the whole point of the rule this tree runs on.

She was born about 1877 and married in December 1899 at roughly twenty-two. Somewhere in the 1880
census she is a three-year-old under a surname that one of these two records got wrong, or under a
spelling no index has normalised. That is where she will be found, and it will take reading pages
rather than searching them.

## The Coffee County marriage books — browsable, but not in order

Worth recording as method, because an hour went into learning it.

The Georgia Archives *countyfilm* collection holds the actual marriage books as scanned images.
**Coffee County Marriage Book 1897–1900 is compound-object pointer 322713**; its first page is
pointer 322455, and pointers increment by one per two-page spread. The pages can be pulled at full
resolution over IIIF without a browser.

But the book has **no alphabetical index**, and — the part that matters — **it is not
chronological**. Page 450 is October 1901; pages 274–281 cover December 1899 and January 1900.
Returns were bound as they came back from the ministers and JPs, not as the marriages happened, so
a December 1899 licence can sit anywhere. Walking the whole book is possible but expensive.

The cheaper route won: the **FamilySearch county marriage index** carries the same entry in
searchable form, and that index is built from these same films. When a marriage is needed, search
the index first and only go to the film when the index is silent or the entry needs its own words
read.

## Joshua Carver's father — the "Joe Carver" question is closed, and the answer is no

Elias Carver's father was confirmed last pass as **Joshua Carver** (1880 census, District 1026 —
the first census to record relationship to head). The obvious next move was to hang Joshua on
**James Carver**, because Uncle Allen Carver's 1916 obituary lists his surviving brothers and one
of them is a **"Joe Carver"** — and "Joe" for "Joshua" is not a stretch.

**It is not him.** A **Joseph Carver, born 17 August 1847, died 16 January 1916**, lies buried in
Coffee County — dying within a day or two of the notice that named him among the living, which is
exactly how obituary brother-lists are usually compiled. He is three years older than Joshua and a
different man. The obituary's Joe is accounted for.

So **Joshua should not be hung on James Carver**, and the guess has been left off the tree.

### What is known about Joshua's origins, and where the father actually is

The 1880 census of District 1026 holds **Pheby Sping, born about 1820 in Georgia**, with two sons
under two different surnames: **Mathew Sping (b. 1853)** and **Joshua Carver (b. 1850)**.

Read plainly: **Joshua's father was a Carver who died or left before about 1852, and Pheby then
married a man named Sping** — almost certainly *Spring*, mangled by an enumerator or an indexer.
That is the shape of it, and it explains why Joshua is not in any Carver household as a boy.

Pheby cannot be found in the 1870 or 1860 indexes under that surname, which is unsurprising given
what the name has already done to itself once.

**Joshua is in the 1860 census, aged ten, in the 3rd District, 748th Georgia Militia District,
Coffee County — alongside sixty-one other Carvers**, among them **Allen Carver, 15** and **James
Carver, 45**. He is in the middle of a Carver settlement, which is the good news and the bad news

## The 1880 household, read properly this time

in the household", which is all the site claimed before.

## Her surname is Spivey, not Sping — corrected on the site

The 1880 index reads the surname as *Sping*, and this tree repeated it, guessing at "Spring". Both were wrong.

The **1860 census of the 4th District, 1030 Georgia Militia, Coffee County, page 35** holds:

> **Phebia Spivy, 38** · Columbus Spivy, 15 · **Mathew Spivy, 8** · **Maranda Spivy, 1**

**Mathew at eight in 1860 is Mathew at twenty-seven in 1880.** The same boy ties the two sheets together and
fixes the spelling in the earlier, more legible hand. No husband is entered with her in 1860 — she heads the
household — so the Spivey she married was dead or gone by then.

Two surnames among her children tells the story: Joshua born a **Carver** in 1850, Mathew born a **Spivey**
about 1852. A first husband named Carver who died or left inside two years of Joshua's birth, and a second
named Spivey. That is why Joshua's father has no name on any surviving sheet.

**Pheby Spivey is now on the tree** as generation VII, drawn to Joshua — but see below for why the link is
drawn *probable* and not *confirmed*.

## Joshua did not grow up with his mother — and that is the problem

Two censuses put him in the same house, and it is not hers.

**1870, Division 31, Coffee County, household 211, page 35, line 17:** John Carver 50 (head), Martha Carver 50,
Margaret 22, **Joshua 20**, Jesse 18, Maryan 16, Saryan 16, Martha 14, Eady 12, Elizer 11, Emilia 9.

**1860, 3rd District 748 Georgia Militia:** John 40, Martha 37, Margaret 12, **Joshua 10**, Jessee 8, Mary 6,
Sary 6, Martha 4, Eady 2, Elizia 1.

**Eight names match across the two pages, aged exactly ten years.** Joshua sits in the middle of that birth
order — two years under Margaret, two over Jesse — precisely where a son belongs.

But **Martha cannot be his mother, because Pheby is.**

### Reading one — the 1880 sheet is literal

Pheby Spivey is his mother; he was born to her in 1850 by a Carver; he was raised among his father's people,
in John Carver's house, with Martha as a stepmother or an aunt. On this reading **John Carver is very probably
his father**, which is exactly why the boy was under his roof for twenty years, and Mathew Spivey is a
half-brother.

### Reading two — the enumerator dropped "in law"

He wrote *Mother* and *Brother* where he meant mother-in-law and brother-in-law, which happened often. This
reading has one uncomfortably good piece of support: **Maranda Spivy, aged 1 in 1860, was born in 1859 — and
Joshua's wife Amanda is 21 in 1880, born 1859.** If Amanda is Pheby's daughter Maranda, then Pheby is his
mother-in-law, Mathew his brother-in-law, and Joshua is simply John and Martha's son.

### Where it was left

**Reading one is preferred, and only just**, because it is the one an actual relationship column supports;
reading two rests on a shared birth year and a common given name. So Pheby is drawn **probable**, and **John
and Martha Carver are named on Joshua's page but not drawn at all.**

**One page settles it.** The 1880 original for District 1026, sheet 238D, either says *Mother* or says
*Mother in law*. Reading that one column is the entire job, and it is the single highest-value next step on
this branch.

- **Elias Carver's dates are now firm**: born 20 March 1877, died **13 August 1954**, buried Carver Baptist
  Church Cemetery, Douglas. His death falls outside *both* free Georgia certificate windows (1919–27 and

Across four records Elias's wife is never "Laura": **"Larceny" (1920 census), "Lucine" (1930 census),

## Method note

FamilySearch groups 1850, 1860 and 1870 households by dwelling/family number even though those censuses
recorded no relationships. **"Other People on This Record" on a record detail page gives the whole household
for free** — which is how the John Carver household was read without ever opening an image. For 1880 and
later the relationship column is indexed too and appears as "Parents and Siblings" / "Spouses and Children".
Open the record detail page, not just the search results row: the search summary omits ages and relationships.

# Eighth pass — 17 September 2026, late
### Laura: a household found by dropping her given name

The previous passes searched for *Laura Story, born about 1877* and found nothing anywhere in Georgia. That
was the wrong search. Dropping the given name and searching the **surname alone in the county** produced a
result immediately.

**There is exactly one Story household in the whole of Coffee County in 1880, and it is in District 1026** —
Elias Carver's own district, the same page-range that holds his father Joshua.

> **Joseph Story, 35** · **Jeney Story, 42** · Jacob 18 · Cassy 14 · **Josiah 8** · Jeney 5 ·
> **Marcenia, 3 (born 1877)** · Sarah C. 1

**Marcenia is the only child of 1877 in the only Story family in the county.** Laura was born 5 September 1877.

## Why "Marcenia" and "Laura" may be the same woman

Elias's wife is written four different ways across four records:

| Record | Name |
|---|---|
| 1899 Coffee County marriage return | **Laura** Story |
| 1920 census | **Larceny** Carver |
| 1930 census | **Lucine** Carver |
| US Cemetery Abstracts 1949–69 | **Lareen** Carver |
| 1950 census | **Laura** Carver |

*Larceny*, *Lucine* and *Lareen* are not anybody's rendering of **Laura**. They are all plausible manglings of
something like **Larcenia** or **Lorena** — and **Larcenia and Marcenia differ by one capital letter** in a
hand where L and M are routinely confused. A woman formally christened Larcenia and called Laura would
produce exactly this scatter.

## The memorial was working from something after all

It gave her parents as *Josiah Story and Jincy Willis*. Previous passes wrote that couple off as unsupported
because no such household existed. Look again at this one:

- It contains a **Josiah Story** — aged 8, so her **brother**, not her father.
- The mother is entered as **Jeney**, which is how a clerk writes **Jincy**.

So the memorial took a son's name for the father's and got the mother right. The actual father would be
**Joseph**. That is a partial rehabilitation of a source this tree had discarded, and it is worth recording
as a caution: a garbled memorial is not the same as a fabricated one.

## Not drawn, and what would settle it

Joseph and Jeney Story are **named on Laura's page and nowhere else** — no person records, no parent link.
Three things would settle it, in order of cost:

1. **Her own 1958 death certificate** — names both parents; orderable from the Georgia Archives (her death
   falls outside both free windows).
2. **The 1900 census** for the Story household and for the newly married Carvers.
3. **The name cut on her headstone** at Carver Baptist Church Cemetery.

## A loose thread on Newbern

Jeney is **seven years older than Joseph**, and her eldest, Jacob, was born when Joseph was seventeen. That
is the ordinary signature of a second marriage — an older widow with children, remarried to a younger man,
her first family enumerated under his surname. **If her first husband was a Newbern, the two competing maiden
names on Laura's page stop competing.** Recorded as a guess, not a finding.

## Negative from this pass

- **No Elias Carver in Coffee County in the 1900 census** under that spelling. He married in December 1899, so
  he is there under something else — worth finding, because that page carries his wife's name and birth month.

> **Father: Jake Hall**

**Not Jesse.** The compiled tree LRQG-B4J gives this family's father as *Jesse Hall, 1821–1898*. A state
death certificate, filled in by the man's own family, says **Jake**.

That is exactly the kind of collision this tree exists to catch, and it goes the good way: **a record beats
an online tree.**

## And Jake is findable

No **Jake Hall** heads a household anywhere in Clinch County. But there is:

> **Jacob N. Hall, born 1851** — 1910 census, **Militia District 586, Clinch County**, wife **Alice A.**
> Plus two **Clinch County voter registrations**.

**Militia District 586 is Henry's own district** — where he appears in 1900, 1910 and 1920. *Jake* is the
ordinary short form of *Jacob*, and he is the only Jacob Hall in the county.

## The reconstruction, and its holes

Jacob "Jake" N. Hall (b. ~1851) and a wife **Jane** (b. ~1845) raising Joseph, Jessee, Rosa Lee, **Preston**,
Rosetta and **Henry B.** in Clinch County. By 1910 Jane is gone and Jacob has married Alice — and the
compiled tree puts Jane Brown's death at 1885, which fits that.

Both men this tree cares about, Henry and Preston, are in that single 1880 household, and the only father's
name any record supplies for either is **Jake**.

**Not drawn. The holes, named:**

1. A Jacob born 1851 cannot father a Joseph born 1860. Either Joseph is Jane's by an earlier husband, or
   his age is wrong.
2. **No record yet read puts Jacob and Jane under one roof.**
3. Reading *Jake* as *Jacob N.* is an identification of a nickname, however well it fits.

What this pass actually achieved: **a compiled tree's assertion has been replaced by a state record that
contradicts it**, and the search now points at a man who can be found in censuses, voter rolls and tax
digests rather than at a name that appears only in other people's trees.

## Also this pass

- **Preston Hall married Rochel Wilson, Clinch County, 10 June 1906** — a Hall–Wilson marriage in the right
  family and county. Henry's wife is claimed (tree only) to be a **Wilson**. Two brothers marrying two
  Wilson women would echo the two Carver sisters who married two McKinnon men already on this site. Worth
  chasing: Rochel/Recy Wilson's family may be Louriday's.
- **No Wilsons at all index in Clinch County for 1900**, which blocked the direct approach to Louriday's
  family. Another 1900-era indexing hole, alongside the missing Storys in Coffee and the missing Elias
  Carver.
- **No Colonel Hall in Atkinson in 1930**, so the tree's claim that Henry was at Axson in 1930 — which
  would put father and son in the same settlement — remains untested.

## State of the tree

**60 people · 33 parent links · 21 documents · 21 census households · ten generations.**

# Thirteenth pass — Jacob next door, and a father who was not at home

## Jacob N. Hall and Henry Hall were neighbours

The 1910 census of **Militia District 586, ED 32, Clinch County**:

- **Henry Hall — sheet 4B** (with Gurdy, Analaura, Jesse, Henry, Colonel)
- **Jacob N. Hall — sheet 5A** (age 59, with wife **Alice A.**, 58, and nobody else)

**Consecutive pages.** The enumerator walked out of one house and into the other. An old couple living next
door to a young family of the same surname is not proof, but it is exactly the arrangement expected if
Jacob is Henry's father — and it is the first physical connection between them beyond a shared district
number.

## The 1880 census read out in full — and it complicates the story

There are only **twenty-two Hall records in the whole of Clinch County in 1880**, in three households:

1. **Jane Hall, District 1061** — head, b. 1845, with Joseph 20, Jessee 16, Rosa Lee 12, Preston 10,
   Rosetta 8, Henry 3
2. **Abiga Hall & Catherine Hall, Magnolia** — with Rufus F., James W., Lurana, Pollie R., Emma Deloach
3. Scattered singles — Moses (District 1219), Theodotia, George W. (Magnolia)

**Jane heads hers alone. There is no husband anywhere on the page.**

That matters, because **Jacob N. Hall was alive in 1880** — he is 59 in 1910, so 29 in 1880. A father who is
alive and not in the house is a separation, a work absence, or a man the enumerator missed. It is *not* the
ordinary shape of a widow's household, which is what Jane's looks like.

## Where that leaves it

Three facts that fit together, but not tidily:

1. A **state death certificate** names Preston's father as **Jake**.
2. The **only Jacob Hall in the county** lived **next door to Henry** in 1910.
3. The **1880 household** those two boys grew up in **had no father in it**, and Jacob was alive.

Recorded on the site without forcing them into a line.

## Negatives from this pass

- **No Jacob Hall in Clinch County in the 1880 census** under that name — so if he was the father, he was
  enumerated elsewhere, under initials, or missed.
- **No death record for Jacob N. Hall in Clinch County** in any indexed series, so he probably died after
  1927 (outside the free window) or outside the county. His certificate would name his own parents and add
  a generation.
- Jacob and Alice are **alone** in 1910 — no children in the house to cross-reference.

## Next moves on this branch, ranked

1. **Henry B. Hall's 1956 Lanier County death certificate** — names both parents outright. Orderable.
   Still the single highest-value purchase on the tree.
2. **Jacob N. Hall in the 1900 census** — the 1900 schedule asks years married and, for the wife, children
   born and living. That would date the end of Jane and the start of Alice, and test the whole
   reconstruction cheaply.
3. **The Clinch County tax digests and voter registrations** already located for both Henry B. and Jacob N.
   — index entries only so far.
4. **Rochel/Recy Wilson's family**, which may also be Louriday's.

# Fourteenth pass — a published negative retracted, and the memorial partly vindicated

## The error

The eighth pass and Elias Carver's page both carried this claim:

> "…nor, for that matter, a single **Story or Storey household in the whole county** [in 1900]… The bride's
> family and the couple both drop out of the 1900 index together."

**That was false, and it was my error, not the record's.**

**FamilySearch's 1900 collection (id 1325221) silently returns zero results when a surname is supplied
without a given name.** The same query shape works fine against 1880 and 1910. Two "clean negatives" were
built on that broken filter and written onto the live site as findings.

## What is actually there

> **Joseph Story**, b. **January 1872** — 1900 census, **Militia District 748, Douglas town, ED 20, Coffee
> County**, sheet 9B, household 171 · married **11 years** · wife **Mary**, 26 · **Sarah 8**, **Ginney 5**,
> **Allen 2**

The Storys never left Coffee County. **Elias Carver is the one missing from 1900 — not his wife's people.**
The site has been corrected in place, with the mistake left visible rather than quietly edited out.

Also retracted by implication: **"no Wilsons at all index in Clinch County for 1900"** (thirteenth pass) was
produced by the identical broken query shape and should not be relied on.

## And the memorial gets partly vindicated

The eighth pass concluded that Laura's memorial had "taken a son's name for the father's" — because it named
her father as *Josiah Story* while the 1880 household head is *Joseph Story, b. 1845*, with a son *Josiah,
aged 8*.

**That son turns up in 1900 indexed as *Joseph*.** Same man, both names, and FamilySearch carries both
records under one person (Josiah S Story, 1871–1906). **This family used Joseph and Josiah interchangeably.**

So if the father was also known by both, then **"Josiah Story" is simply his name** and the memorial points
straight at the head of the only Story household in the county — no garbling required. That materially
strengthens the Marcenia/Laura identification rather than weakening it.

The naming runs on: the 1872 son named his own daughter **Ginney**, after the **Jeney** who is her
grandmother on the 1880 page.

## Method note — add to the standing list

**Always supply a given name when searching the 1900 census collection.** A surname-plus-county query
returns zero and looks like a clean negative. This has now produced at least two false findings on this
site; any 1900-era negative recorded before this pass should be re-run before being trusted.

Running list of query shapes that silently return zero:

- `q.surname` + `q.residencePlace` + `f.collectionId=1325221` (1900) — **needs a given name**
- `q.birthLikePlace` + `f.collectionId` (any)
- `q.birthLikeDate` with no collection filter
- `q.deathLikeDate` in any combination tried
- `~` fuzzy on a given name combined with any date filter
- `q.surname.exact=on`
- `q.residencePlace` at county level for 1930 Atkinson, though records exist

# Fifteenth pass — a third brother, and the whole family moves at once

## Talley H. Hall is Henry's brother, by the relationship column

The man Henry was boarding with in the 1900 census is not a landlord. **Henry is entered against
*Brother*** to the head, **Talley H. Hall** — the census stating outright that the two are brothers.

**Talley Howard Hall, Sr.**
- Born **27 August 1874**; died **10 July 1929**
- Buried **Springhead Methodist Church Cemetery, Willacoochee, Atkinson County**
- Married **Mary E. Tyson**
- **1910 census, Pearson** (then still Coffee County) with Bula E., Mamie, Mary, Matie, Maggie, Johnnie, Robbie
- WWI draft registration, Georgia, b. 27 August 1874
- Son **Talley Howard Hall, Jr.**, b. 12 July 1914 at Willacoochee, d. 30 August 1989, buried beside him

## The structural finding: this was a family relocation

Three brothers, one road out of Clinch County into the same few miles of Atkinson:

| Brother | Where he lands |
|---|---|
| **Henry B.** | Axson, then the Lax country |
| **Preston** | dies at **Pearson**, 1924 |
| **Talley** | farming at **Pearson** by 1910; buried **Willacoochee**, 1929 |

**The move out of Clinch was not one man leaving home — it was a family relocating together**, into exactly
the ground where Jesse Lee Hall would marry, farm and be buried, and where Sally lives now.

That also reframes Jesse's own move from Mud Creek to Axson: he did not strike out on his own, he moved with
(or after) uncles who were already there.

## It argues against the headstone

In 1900 Talley is **26** and heads the house; Henry is **24** and single under his roof. That is the ordinary
arrangement of a younger brother lodging with an older one, and it fits a Henry born in **1876**.

A Henry born in **1867** would be **seven years older than the brother he was living under**, still unmarried
at thirty-three. Not impossible — but the 1876 reading makes an ordinary household, and **the Mud Creek stone
is looking more like the outlier** among his records.

## Negatives from this pass

- **No Jacob Hall in Clinch County in the 1900 census** — searched correctly this time, with a given name
  supplied. So Jacob is in Clinch in 1910 but not indexed there in 1880 or 1900.
- **Talley's own 1929 death certificate is not in the digitised *Georgia Deaths 1928–1943* index.** He appears
  there only as *father* on his daughter **Maggie Newmans'** certificate. Confirms that the 1928–43 series is
  partial in the same way the 1919–27 series is — a second brother's certificate would have named the parents
  and cross-checked "Jake."

## Where the parents question stands

Two of the three brothers can be reached in free state records, and between them they yield exactly one
parental name: **Jake**, from Preston's 1924 certificate, with the mother's box left blank.

**Henry B. Hall's 1956 Lanier County certificate remains the buy.** Everything else on this branch is now
downstream of it.

# Sixteenth pass — Luridy's maiden name proved, and a new generation drawn

## The retraction paid for itself immediately

The fourteenth pass retracted a false negative caused by surname-only searches silently failing on the 1900
collection — including **"no Wilsons at all index in Clinch County for 1900."** Re-running that search
*correctly*, with a given name supplied, found the family on the first try.

## The household

> **1900 census — Militia District 586, Mud Creek, ED 14, Clinch County — household 101, sheet 6A**
>
> **John M. Wilson, Jr.**, Head, **60**, b. February 1840, Georgia — **married 30 years**
> **Helan Wilson**, wife, **50**, b. **South Carolina**
> **Rachael**, daughter, 24, single, b. November 1876
> **Lurrida**, 18
> **Lilla**, 15 · **Joseph**, 13 · **Franklin**, 8

## Why Lurrida is Luridy — and why it is drawn as confirmed

1. **The name.** Fifth spelling of a distinctive name: *Gurdy* (1910), *Luridy* (1920), *Lundy* (WWI card),
   *Louriday* (compiled tree), **Lurrida** (1900).
2. **The age.** Eighteen in 1900 → born about **1882**, against 1884 in 1910 and 1883 in 1920 — as close as
   census ages ever come.
3. **The place.** She is at **Mud Creek, Militia District 586** — the exact locality and district where
   Henry Hall was living that same summer, in the same enumeration district.
4. **The relationship column** enters her as a daughter of that house.
5. **The cross-link.** Her sister **Rachael**, single and 24 on this sheet, married **Preston Hall** — Henry's
   brother — in Clinch County on **10 June 1906**.

**Two Hall brothers married two Wilson sisters.** That is the second time this tree has caught the pattern:
two Carver sisters married two McKinnon men, three generations later and forty miles west.

**John M. Wilson, Jr. and Helan Wilson are added as generation VI and drawn to Luridy as confirmed.**
Luridy is renamed from *Luridy Hall* to **Luridy Wilson** and her birth revised to about 1882.

## Two threads this opens

- **"Junior."** A man calling himself *Jr.* at sixty had a father of the same name — a **John M. Wilson, Sr.**
  of Clinch County, born perhaps in the 1810s. Untaken.
- **South Carolina.** Helan is the first South Carolinian on this side of the tree, while her husband and
  every one of her children is Georgia-born. That single column is a migration, and the Hughses on another
  branch came the same way. Her maiden name is not on the sheet; the **Clinch County marriage books for about
  1870** — the couple married thirty years before 1900 — would have it.

## A note on the compiled tree

The tree's claim that Luridy was a Wilson turns out to be **correct**. That is worth saying plainly, and so
is the other half: **a tree being right is not the same as a tree being evidence.** The page now rests on a
census household, not on someone else's assertion, and the two sisters' marriages are the kind of
corroboration no tree supplied.

# Seventeenth pass — a Confederate pension, and the "Junior" turns out to be a red herring

## The Junior is not a Junior in the genealogical sense

The sixteenth pass proposed a thread: *John M. Wilson **Jr.** at sixty implies a **John M. Wilson Sr.** of
Clinch County, born perhaps in the 1810s.* **That reading was wrong.**

There is indeed another John M. Wilson in Clinch County — in the same 1900 census, at **Du Pont**, with a
wife **Martha** and children **Charley** and **Iola**. The county voter rolls carry both men, one registered
in **1889** and one in **1894**, one docketed *Sr.* and one *Jr.*

**But the older man was born in February 1836 — four years before ours (February 1840).** A father cannot be
four years older than his son.

**These two are not father and son.** In a county holding two men of one name, the clerk wrote *Sr.* against
the elder and *Jr.* against the younger. **The suffix is a filing convenience, not a pedigree** — and a tree
that reads it as descent invents a generation that never existed. Worth keeping as a standing caution.

## He was a Confederate soldier

> **Georgia Confederate Pension Rolls, 1861–1921** — **John M. Wilson**, **Company H, 26th Georgia**,
> Clinch County — record also names **Hellen Wilson**

**Hellen is what ties the file to this house** rather than to the other John M. Wilson down the road. He was
twenty-one in 1861.

Georgia paid these pensions to veterans who could demonstrate need, and later to their widows, so the
application is a document about poverty as much as about service.

**The file itself is at the Georgia Archives and has not been read.** Applications of this kind normally
carry a sworn statement of service, a residence, sometimes a physical description, and the names of men who
vouched for the applicant — considerably more of a man than a census line gives. **Second on the purchase
list after Henry B. Hall's 1956 death certificate.**

## They were newcomers to Clinch County

**The 1880 census holds exactly one Wilson household in the whole county** — Anthoney and Annie *Wilsin* at
Homerville with four small children, and it is not this family.

Rachael was born in Georgia in 1876 and Lurrida in 1882, so the Wilsons were in the state but living
somewhere else. **They arrived in Clinch between 1880 and 1900**, and where they came from is now the open
question on this line.

Helan's South Carolina birth (about 1850) is the other half of that migration.

## Standing caution added

**"Sr." and "Jr." in nineteenth-century Southern county records frequently distinguish two unrelated men of
the same name by age, not by descent.** Check birth years before drawing a generation from a suffix.

# Eighteenth pass — the pension file pulled, read, and hosted

## It was findable after all

The seventeenth pass said the pension file was "at the Georgia Archives and has not been read." It is in fact
**digitised and free**. The Georgia Archives Virtual Vault holds **Confederate Pension Applications** under
the collection alias **`TestApps`** (and supplements under `pension`), reachable by the same CONTENTdm API and
IIIF endpoints already used on this project for `gadeaths` and `countyfilm`.

Searching Clinch County Wilsons returned **two** files:

| Pointer | Title |
|---|---|
| 444820 | Wilson, John M |
| **439302** | **Wilson, Helen** |

## First: a correction to my own last pass

The seventeenth pass wrote that the Georgia Confederate Pension Rolls entry — *John M. Wilson, Co. H, 26th
Georgia, Clinch*, with *Hellen Wilson* attached — tied the file to our house.

**Reading the actual application shows it does not.** File 444820 is the **other** John M. Wilson: Q1 gives
his residence as **Du Pont**, Q3 his birth as **1836**, Q19 his wife's age as **seventy**. That is the man
with wife Martha, not ours with wife Helan.

**The trap was the exact one the same pass had just described** — two men of one name in one county, and an
index that does not distinguish them. Corrected on the site, with the error left visible.

## What our man's record actually says

From **Helen Wilson's widow's application, sworn 4 October 1913** before J. T. Dame, Ordinary of Clinch County:

- Widow of **John M. Wilson**
- Married in Clinch County on the **8th day of August 1867**
- He died **13 September 1912**; she had not remarried
- He was on the **indigent** pension roll, paid **$60.00** for 1912
- Service: **Company H, 29th Regiment, Georgia**
- His property at death: **none**, cash value **none**
- Her own property: **15 head of cattle, $150.00**, every other line blank
- Signed **Hellen ✗ Wilson — her mark**
- Marriage witness: **Harris Sirmans**, sworn the same day

**John M. Wilson's death date (13 September 1912) is new** and is now on the tree.

**Regiment unresolved:** the widow's affidavit says **29th**; the other man's file and the FamilySearch index
say **26th**. The site carries the 29th — the number on the document about *him* — and flags that it is not
perfectly clean.

## The finding that outlives this family

Written across the foot of the last page in the clerk's hand:

> **"No record of Marriage. Records all destroyed by fire in 1868."**

**Clinch County's records burned in 1868.** That is why a marriage of August 1867 had to be proved by a
neighbour swearing from memory — and it is why every search on this branch thins the moment it crosses back
before 1870. **A structural fact about the county, not about the people**, and it should be assumed in all
future Clinch work.

The statute is why the date was fought over at all: Georgia pensioned only widows **married before 1 January
1870**. Her claim turned on proving a wedding two and a half years inside that line with no paper left.

## One contradiction, recorded not resolved

She swore she had resided in Clinch County **"all her life."** The 1900 enumerator gave her birthplace as
**South Carolina**. Printed forms invite that shorthand and so do tired enumerators. The page records both
and settles neither.

## Added to the site

**`d-helen-wilson-widow`** — the widow's affidavit, embedded and readable in place, with the full transcript
of what the columns say. Documents now stand at **22**.

## Method note

**Georgia Confederate Pension Applications are free and digitised.** Collection alias `TestApps` at
vault.georgiaarchives.org; supplements at `pension`. Search by surname, filter on the `covera` field for the
county, then `dmGetCompoundObjectInfo` for pages and IIIF for images. **Widows' applications are the richer
document** — they carry a marriage date, a death date, and a property schedule that a veteran's own
application does not.
