How to read this. The colour on the left edge of every card is the evidence grade, not decoration. Green means a document says so. Amber means the case is strong but circumstantial. Rust means somebody's online tree says so and nothing else does — those are research targets, not facts.

Click any name for their dates, the census households they appear in, and the exact records behind every claim.

What the federal censuses settled. Jack Luke's parentage is proven twice over (1900 and 1910). Monroe McKinnon's is proven by a 1910 page on which the enumerator wrote maternal grandfather beside John Rickerson — which is what turns the Ricketson name from a tree claim into a fact. Nealie Hinton's mother is confirmed in Levy County, Florida. R. B. Luke's parents are confirmed in Berrien County. And both missing sisters were found — Dorothy in the 1930 household, born in Florida about 1927.

The fifteen children of Jack & Nealie Luke

1920 – 1949
Brenda's 2023 obituary lists six surviving siblings and says she was preceded by eight — six plus eight plus Brenda is fifteen. But the fifteenth isn't Nealie's: Cleo was a half-brother, son of Jack's first wife Clyde Elizabeth McMillan, whom he married in 1918. The true count is fourteen by Nealie, plus Cleo.

The twelve children of Monroe & Mary Jane McKinnon

1924 – 1945
All twelve appear together in the 1950 census at Mora, or are named in their siblings' obituaries. Five separate funeral notices name Monroe McKinnon Sr. and Mary Jane Carver McKinnon as the parents — the best-documented couple in the whole tree.
